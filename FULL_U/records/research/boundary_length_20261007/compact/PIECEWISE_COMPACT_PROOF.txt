PIECEWISE COMPACT COERCIVITY, ANGLES85 AND255

Each witness's actual objective is its exact binary64 pair, interpreted as
rationals; the nominal degree label is only a name. All physical/source
parameters retain the canonical exact conventions B=119/10 and c=T=4/3.

The claim to assemble is V_l(mu)/p_l(mu)<-1/4 for every even l>=42 and
4<mu<=49. This is only a compact-energy sign component of a support bound.
The finite head, real norms and UV sign components are separate.

ANALYTIC RECTANGLES
The positive Legendre series provides a probability measure on k. For85,
the exact Bernstein bound on the shifted crossing polynomial retains its
negative fifth-power coefficient. For255 all negative shifted powers j>=1
are retained. If their nonnegative magnitudes are a_j and M bounds k<10,
then C(k,z)<=M-sum a_j*(k-10)_+^j. Every retained function is convex,
so E C<=M-sum a_j*(E k-10)_+^j. The constant shifted coefficient is safely
dropped. No convexity of C itself is assumed.

On a low-energy rectangle with upper endpoint b<=8, use Q<=b-c and
Delta<=b-4. The reviewed source bound is a constant plus a nonnegative
multiple of l. The forward ratio is bounded by a positive denominator
monomial; E k/l is bounded below by its value at the selected cutoff L
and Delta=b-4. The exact derivative of the resulting linear-minus-convex
upper envelope is checked negative at L; it remains negative above L.

On a physical-source rectangle [a,b] with a>=8, retain the original
conservative source constants 1/8,1/4 and dmin=83/60. Only Qmin=a-c,
Qmax=b-c and DeltaMax=b-4 replace the old global endpoints. Each forward
or source numerator has degree at most3 in l(l+1). The selected positive
Legendre denominator monomial has degree K>=4; its ratio with each such
numerator decreases with l. The mean increases with l and decreases with
Delta, so its cutoff value at DeltaMax bounds the entire rectangle.
These are domain restrictions of the reviewed estimates, not new sampling
approximations. Every chosen analytic upper is strictly below-1; the
assembled declared coercivity is the weaker exact1/4.

FINITE RECTANGLES
For an analytic cutoff L, the complementary finite spins are exactly
42,44,...,L-2. Their rational/polynomial source contractions use exact-degree
Gauss moment enclosures, explicit geometric remainders, removable-pole
division, and tapered principal-value endpoint formulas. The same reviewed
evaluator is used at512 and640bits. Every accepted interval must be finite
and have directed upper endpoint<-1/4. Replays evaluate every original
spin/energy cell; they do not merely compare floating midpoints.
The exact per-spin interval endpoints are checked adjacent and gap-free.

Angle85 retains already completed broad finite rectangles8..12 and12..24
through spin110, using the original analytic cutoff112. All remaining
rectangles use the smaller piecewise cutoffs. Angle255 uses piecewise
cutoffs throughout; rectangles8..10 and10..12 need no finite bridge because
the analytic cutoff is42. Its allspin42 low interval ends at9/2, while85's
ends at19/4. Adjacent closed proofs overlap only at their energy endpoints.

ASSEMBLY
assemble_angle85.py and assemble_angle255.py verify each producer/replay
hash, exact witness, objective, precision, strict coercivity, finite spin
list and interval partition. They pair analytic cutoffs with complementary
finite ranges, verify exact energy adjacency from4 to49, bind independent
source-moment audits and the parent's structural review, and write a
complete_compact_coercivity.json only after every component exists and
passes. The endpoint4 is explicitly open.

REPLAY LIMITS
Higher precision shares the mathematical evaluator; this is not an
independent implementation or proof-assistant proof. Independent floating
kernel comparisons are diagnostics and are not used as interval proofs.
Independent exact beta identities and source moment overlaps give a second
construction check of the contracted inputs. The entire argument retains
the dispersion-relation and amplitude-class assumptions of the main work.

RESOURCE SETTINGS
Every new worker sets OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=OMP_NUM_THREADS=MKL_NUM_THREADS=1.
The85 launcher uses at most2 workers; after those finish and resources free,
the255 launcher uses at most4 workers. Earlier broad jobs exhausted Windows
virtual commit through large native thread stacks and were abandoned before
producing certificates. Their incomplete logs are diagnostics, not inputs.
One new replay wrapper initially failed before evaluation due to an undefined
local name in its spin-list assertion. It was repaired before any successful
local replay artifact was produced; successful artifacts bind repaired code.

REPRODUCTION ORDER (on a disposable copy if preserving frozen artifacts)
1. Verify witness/source-cache manifests.
2. Build contracted moment tables with the corresponding builder at512bits;
   independently audit the tables against exact beta identities/head tables.
3. Run analytic_angle85.py --bits400/512 --L112 --low-endpoint19/4,
   analytic_piecewise.py --bits400/512 for85; or analytic_angle255.py and
   analytic_angle255_low.py at400/512 for255.
4. For each finite rectangle, run certify_finite_slice.py (85) or
   certify_finite255_slice.py (255), with exact --mu-min/--mu-max,
   --lmax L-2 --bits512. A prior partition is only a seed; all values are new.
5. Replay each resulting file using replay_piecewise.py (85 local) or
   replay_piecewise255.py, --bits640 --jobs1. The older full85 rectangles use
   replay_finite_parallel.py with their full42..110 spin list.
6. Run the exact component assembler. The complete packet includes the
   explicit rectangle coverage and all input/source SHA256 values.

The run_piecewise255 launcher queues its work after the85 compact packet to
avoid overlapping heavy workers. This scheduling dependency has no role in
the mathematical proof and is unnecessary for a standalone255 replay.
