normal cones are never interpolated into extra coverage.

For each observed reference point p, n is its outward unit normal in the
selected metric. The physical normal ray is p+t*diag(sx,sy)*n. Intersecting
that line with every exact-source certified half-plane gives an interval in
t. The upper endpoint is the signed local normal-line gap: positive means
the certified polygon still extends beyond the reference along that ray;
negative means the reference is already cut away. If the line misses the
polygon entirely, its gap is null and that case is flagged. A separate
signed nearest-boundary distance remains available. The latter is positive
inside the polygon and negative outside. Neither quantity is the support
function difference, and neither interprets a corner normal cone as a long
piece of boundary.

Reference arclength excluded by the current polygon is computed by clipping
each observed line segment against all half-planes. The unexcluded parameter
interval is multiplied by that segment's selected-metric length. This is a
diagnostic of digitized geometry, not a rigorous error-controlled comparison
to the published optimizer. Its complement is the observed reference length
still lying inside the new outer polygon; it does not measure parameter-space
area, the length of the green polygon, or proof completeness.
