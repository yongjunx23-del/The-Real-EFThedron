VALID CONSTRAINTS AND IMPLEMENTATION NOTES: MASSIVE 4D g0-g2
Independent analysis of The_Real_EFThedron-7.pdf, especially A2 and A5.
2026-10-06. This file is analytical guidance, not a new numerical bound.

1. What has already been imposed
--------------------------------
The manuscript's Figure 4 enclosure states
  -7.81702 <= g0 <= 2.72322,  0 <= g2 <= 1.05800.
These are numerical enclosures with 1e-4 allowances, not interval certificates.
Its spectral integration includes spins 0,2,...,998 plus bounded omitted-spin
remainders; discarding all spins above a finite cutoff would NOT reproduce it.

The equation bank in A5 contains 198 smeared angular equations below s=11.999,
forward harmonics through j=1024 (upper g2) / 2048 (lower g0), 48 rational
forward generators, nine fractional nulls, degree-eight crossing rows, and
finite-point crossing. The final upper-g2 reoptimization varies only eleven
angular source functionals. The final lower-g0 reoptimization fixes the angular
combination and varies forward weights. Thus a complete joint optimization of
the existing 198 angular directions + forward bank is an obvious unexhausted
convex improvement. Adding still higher harmonic order is not the first priority.

The alternate Figure 5 path equations assume analyticity of restricted
crossing-symmetric paths in a cut X plane. The manuscript explicitly identifies
that as an extra assumption not implied by fixed-t twice-subtracted dispersion.
Do not silently combine those constraints into a same-assumption Figure 4 bound.

2. Normalization and exact kernels
----------------------------------
m=1, c=4/3, Q=s-c, rho(s)=sqrt(1-4/s), f_l=r_l+i b_l,
  r_l^2+(b_l-1)^2 <= 1,
  a(s,t)=M(s,t)/(32 pi)=sum_even_l (2l+1) f_l(s) P_l(1+2t/(s-4))/(2 rho(s)),
  dmu_l(s)=(2l+1) ds/(2 pi rho(s)).

Let P_l,*=P_l(1+8/[3(s-4)]) and p_l=P_l,*/Q^3. Then
  g2=sum_l integral dmu_l p_l b_l.
Use the positive p_l weight to control omitted spins, including their real parts
through the disk. At fixed t=c, g0=(512/9)<p_l Im(omega_* f_l)>.
At forward t=0, use the manuscript's A5.3 forward g0 map, which differs from
the symmetric-point harmonic. The two reconstructions must be matched.

The crossed-subtraction dispersion kernel is
  K(v;s,t)=1/(v-s)+1/(v-4+s+t)-1/(v-c)-1/(v-4+t+c).
Define
  L_l(v;s,t)=P_l(1+2t/(v-4)) K(v;s,t)
             +P_l(1+8/[3(v-4)]) K(v;t,c).
Then Re a(s,t)-g0=sum_l integral dmu_l(v) L_l(v;s,t) b_l(v),
with correct real principal values on the physical cut.

For even J and a smooth compactly supported energy weight w(s), set
  D_Jl(v)=integral ds w(s) integral_0^1 2 dz P_J(z) L_l(v;s,t(s,z)),
  t(s,z)=-(s-4)(1-z)/2.
The smeared Roy identity in manuscript normalization is
  integral ds w(s) r_J(s)/rho(s)
       -2 delta_J0 g0 integral ds w(s)
       -sum_l integral dmu_l(v) D_Jl(v) b_l(v)=0.
Hence the LOCAL real-part kernel at spin J is 2 pi w(v)/(2J+1).
Every other spin gets no local real-part term from this individual equation.
Subtract two normalized J=0 reconstructions to make a homogeneous null.

The singularity of D_Jl at v=s is diagonal, so separate the diagonal Hilbert
transform analytically before quadrature. Never approximate a principal value
by simply deleting the closest grid node. For polynomial/Jacobi/spline energy
weights, integrate the singular contribution using exact logs or stable
subtracted quadrature: integral [w(s)-w(v)]/(v-s) plus the known log term.

3. Domain caveat: why s<60 is not automatically an implementation improvement
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The manuscript A2 distinguishes fixed-t validity (-28<t<4), the simple harmonic
coordinate (t>-4), and a conventional folded Roy external range s<60.
However the primary benchmark, arXiv:2605.06613 Appendix A.2, explicitly says
partial-wave convergence and dual feasibility restrict their implemented Roy
kernel expansion to s<=12. Therefore extending its partial-wave-expanded
kernel to s=60 without proving convergence / controlling analytic continuation
is unsafe. Forward-strip smearings at arbitrarily large s are a possible valid
alternative only when the transfer expansion and tails remain controlled.

At t<-4/3, the chosen c=4/3 subtraction lies on a crossed cut and individual
kernel terms acquire poles on v>=4. This is not by itself a failure: in the
combined even-spin L_l, the subtraction-pole residue cancels because at
v=4-c-t, P_l(1+2t/(v-4))=P_l(1+2c/(v-4)) for even l. Evaluate the combined
expression in a cancellation-safe way. Blind separate quadrature is dangerous.

4. Best search strategy: adaptive functionals from exact convex derivatives
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
For a candidate supporting witness let its combined real/imaginary kernels
be U_l,V_l, and R_l=hypot(U_l,V_l). Its cost is
  C=sum_l integral dmu_l [V_l+R_l].
At R_l>0, the maximizing spectrum is exactly
  r_l*=U_l/R_l, b_l*=1+V_l/R_l.
For any additional homogeneous null (u_l,v_l), the cost derivative is
  G=sum_l integral dmu_l [u_l r_l*+v_l b_l*].
The second directional derivative is
  H=sum_l integral dmu_l (u_l V_l-v_l U_l)^2/R_l^3.
Rank optional rows by G^2/H after accounting for conditioning and quadrature;
large nonzero G is direct evidence that the current witness leaves useful
constraint information unused. Reoptimize the best additions, and repeat.
Treat R=0 by subgradient/smoothing only in the optimizer, then evaluate the
unsmoothed disk support when validating. This is a way to compare arbitrary
t samples, derivatives, fractional rows, Roy smearing, and rational resummations
without guessing which family will help.

The cost is convex in all linear null coefficients. Local minima of the exact
convex problem are global, but ill-conditioning and truncated quadrature can
produce misleading numerical termination. Warm-start joint optimization from
the accepted witness, retain it as fallback, and record actual cost decrease.

5. Rational forward rows and energy localization
-----------------------------------------------
At t=0 define F=32/(s-2)^3, omega=exp(i theta),
  cos theta=8/(s-2)^2-1, s=2+2/cos(theta/2).
Each real-coefficient analytic series beginning at omega^2 supplies a null.
For 0<r<1, -1<=c<=1, let
  mc(omega)=(1-r c omega)/(1-2r c omega+r^2 omega^2),
  ms(omega)=r omega/(1-2r c omega+r^2 omega^2).
Use F omega^2 [m(omega)-m(-1)] inside <Im(q f)>; the subtraction improves UV
decay. These are correlated sums of existing harmonic nulls, so require no new
analyticity assumption. The manuscript stops at r=.95. Values .98,.995,.999
provide narrower energy localization and may improve efficiently if placed
where the current extremal spectrum has the largest residual. Refine quadrature
at the corresponding theta peaks; do not rely on a fixed low-order grid.

IMPORTANT: an arbitrary complex pole with arbitrary complex series coefficients
does NOT give a null from Im harmonic moments alone. Use real coefficients,
equivalently conjugate-paired poles. Otherwise one spuriously imposes the real
parts of harmonic moments, which are not zero in the stated hierarchy.

Endpoint-adapted Jacobi weights / compact B-splines in external Roy energy can
resolve localized threshold and resonance structure more efficiently than adding
global Legendre degrees. Use smooth windows and exact PV treatment. Changing
basis alone does not add information, but extending its span can.

6. A simple explicit all-even-spin tail majorant
-----------------------------------------------
For x>=0 and integer l>=2, the positive Legendre expansion gives
  P_l(1+2x)=sum_k binom(l,k) binom(l+k,k) x^k
             >= 1+l^2 x+l^4 x^2/4=(1+l^2 x/2)^2.
The k=2 inequality follows from (l-1)l(l+1)(l+2)>=l^4.

Suppose the omitted-spin imaginary kernel satisfies
  V_l(s)<=-kappa(s) p_l(s), kappa(s)>0,
and the omitted real kernel is forward-only U_l(s)=U(s). Then
  V_l+hypot(U,V_l)<=U(s)^2/[2 kappa(s) p_l(s)].
Consequently, for L=2N>=2,
  tail <= integral_4^infty ds Q^3 U(s)^2/[4 pi rho(s) kappa(s)] * T_N(s),
  T_N(s)=sum_n>=N (4n+1)/P_(2n)(1+2x), x=4/[3(s-4)].
Set a=2x and A=N-1. A rigorous majorant is
  T_N <= integral_A^infty (4u+5)/(1+a u^2)^2 du
       =2/[a(1+a A^2)]
        +(5/[2 sqrt(a)])*[pi/2-arctan(sqrt(a) A)]
        -5 A/[2(1+a A^2)].
Use arctan(1/[sqrt(a) A]) for the bracket when A>0 and large; asymptotic series
or interval evaluation avoids cancellation of its last two terms.
Proof of the sum-integral comparison: on [n-1,n], 4u+5>=4n+1 and
(1+a u^2)^2<=(1+a n^2)^2. Every summand is bounded by its interval integral.

This is deliberately conservative but very easy to implement and valid over
ALL spectral energies. It handles the joint l,s->infinity region, where a fixed
spin asymptotic followed by an energy integral can fail. U~s^-3 already gives
an integrable UV majorant when kappa stays bounded below: T_N=O(s), hence the
integrand is O(s^-2). UV-subtracted forward generators decay still faster.

For derivative or angular real kernels depending on l, increase the retained
positive polynomial order of P_l and majorize U_l^2 accordingly. Do not use the
forward-only formula unless that spin dependence has actually been removed.

A known valid cap g2<=B2 can be included with multiplier eta>=0:
  objective <= eta B2 + disk_support[U, V-eta p].
This may enforce negative tail V-eta p and make the preceding remainder usable.
The manuscript has an interval-certified elementary B2=4.36947880 (A5.25);
using B2=1.05800 inherits that numerical enclosure's non-interval status.
There is no justification for setting the residual positive tail support to zero.

7. Constraints that require extra care or assumptions
---------------------------------------------------
* Complete harmonic families and exact fixed-transfer DRs are equivalent under
  the stated boundary regularity. Resummation improves finite truncations; it
  does not prove information beyond all fixed-transfer dispersion relations.
* Elastic equality below an inelastic threshold needs an assumption about the
  spectrum/channels. The disk inequality alone does not authorize imposing
  equality throughout any numerical interval. Its pointwise convex hull is the
  disk anyway, so nonconvex use needs separate treatment.
* A Froissart asymptotic statement does not supply a universal finite-energy
  cutoff with a known constant. It cannot justify deleting UV contributions.
* Threshold poles / threshold states are present on the benchmark island's
  boundary. Imposing ordinary scattering-length scaling without allowing them
  can spuriously excise physical boundary points.
* Sampling a finite grid or finite spin set gives a candidate calculation, not
  automatically an outer bound. Save the complete witness, check independent
  quadrature, and bound every omitted spin and UV region before reporting an
  improvement over published dual exclusion.

Primary reference checked:
https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06613v1 (especially Appendix A.2, equations 19-26).
The coefficient identification there is exactly (c00,c10)=(g0,g2).

8. Implemented Roy kernel algebra (research/roy_kernels.py)
----------------------------------------------------------
For external s,z, t=-(s-4)(1-z)/2, u=4-s-t; spectral mu, Delta=mu-4,
Q=mu-c, Z=Q/Delta, y=1+2t/Delta. Define the exact polynomial divided difference
  D_l(a,b)=[P_l(a)-P_l(b)]/(a-b),
  D_0=0, D_1=1,
  (l+1)D_(l+1)=(2l+1)[a D_l+P_l(b)]-l D_(l-1).
The apparent subtraction pole d=mu-8/3+t is removable for even l:
  [P_l(Z)-P_l(y)]/d=-(2/Delta)D_l(y,-Z).
The external PV pole can be removed independently:
  P_l(y)/(mu-s)=P_l(z)/(mu-s)+(1-z)D_l(y,z)/Delta.
The angular integral of P_l(z) is diagonal, so for w_n(s) as in section 2 its
entire PV contribution is
  4 delta_Jl Q_n(2(mu-4)/(B-4)-1)/[(2l+1)(B-4)],
where Q_n is the REAL principal-value Legendre function of the second kind.

For large mu, the PV split causes cancellation of UV terms. The code switches
to the following nonsingular factored direct kernel when mu>=B+1:
  L_l/P_l(Z)=L_0 + (2/Delta)[D_l(y,-Z)/P_l(Z)]
                  *(s-c)(c-u)(2mu+t-4)/[(mu-s)Q(mu-u)],
  L_0=[(s-c)^2/(mu-s)+(t-c)^2/(mu-t)+(u-c)^2/(mu-u)]/Q^2.
This exhibits the scalar O(mu^-3) falloff explicitly. It is algebraically the
same combined crossed-subtraction kernel, with no approximation.

Module build_roy_bank(mu,spins,specs,normalized=True) uses specs=(B,J,n),
currently restricts 4<B<12, and returns u,v,g0_coeff,p with rows
  integral dmu [u R+v Babs]+g0_coeff*g0=0,
  R=sqrt(p)r, Babs=p b, g0_coeff=-2 delta_J0 delta_n0.
With normalized=False it returns coefficients of r,b instead.
The local real kernel is nonzero only at spin J and 4<mu<B.

Checks performed: independent divided-difference values and derivatives through
l=20; Legendre-Q against independent cauchy-weight quadrature through n=6;
scalar full rows against independent quadrature; energy/angular refinement from
48x40 to80x64 at l=0..20, J=0,2,4, n=0..3, mu from4.0001 to1e5 changed
normalized kernels by at most1.7e-12 relative to 1+abs(kernel).
These checks validate the implemented finite kernel calculation, not an all-spin
supporting bound or an interval error enclosure.

9. Implemented new finite test directions: high-energy fixed-transfer strips
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
research/transfer_kernels.py exposes build_transfer_bank(mu,spins,specs=None,
normalized=False). Default 36 rows use s windows [12,24] and [24,48], t in
[-4/3,0], energy degrees n=0..5 and transfer degrees j=0..2.
Each explicit spec is (A,B,T,j,n), with 4<A<B and 0<T<=min(4/3,A-4).
The windows are external smearing windows; spectral energy remains all s>4.

Let w_n(s)=P_n(2(s-A)/(B-A)-1)/(B-A),
    h_j(t)=P_j(2(t+T)/T-1)/T,
    H_l(v)=integral_-T^0 dt h_j(t) P_l(1+2t/(v-4)).
Then the real kernel is U_l(v)=pi w_n(v) H_l(v) on A<v<B, and zero otherwise.
The g0 coefficient is -delta_n0 delta_j0.
Unlike folded Roy projection, every even spin has a real kernel.

For n>=1, all subtraction functions vanish upon energy integration. The exact
absorptive source is
  D_l(v)=integral_-T^0 h_j(t)P_l,t [I_n(v)+J_n(v,t)]dt,
  I_n(v)=2 Q_n(2(v-A)/(B-A)-1)/(B-A),
  J_n(v,t)=-2 Q_n(2(4-v-t-A)/(B-A)-1)/(B-A).
For n=0, add the original subtraction terms; the code again uses divided
differences to cancel them before evaluating. Large/small v outside the smearing
window uses the factored direct kernel of section 8 to maintain UV precision.

This does not require extending a full angular Roy projection beyond s=12.
Every external point remains physical and every transfer stays in a fixed safe
strip; it adds nonforward information at high energy to the finite catalogue.
It is a new choice of finite tests of the existing dispersion identity, not a
new axiom and not information beyond the complete dispersion relation.

For |t|<=4/3, |P_l,t|<=P_l,* at every spectral v>4. On the external physical
strip, |H_l(v)|<=integral|h_j|dt<=1, so |U_l(v)|<=pi|w_n(v)| uniformly in l.
This gives a usable spin-independent majorant for the real omitted-spin kernel
in section 6. For a linear combination of strip rows, use pi*sum|lambda_i w_i|;
it has compact energy support and adds no UV convergence problem.

Independent scalar checks: full smeared scalar dispersion kernel and its PV
term matched independent scipy quadrature for j=0..2,n=0..3 and v=4.01..100;
local-real coefficients checked separately. For l=0..20, quadrature refinement
40x32 to80x70 (including v=1e5) changed normalized kernels by <=6.81e-12
relative to1+abs(kernel). No all-spin supporting bound is claimed from this.

10. Critical independent audit after the high-energy-strip numerical improvement
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
research/test_transfer_audit.py and transfer_kernel_audit.json record stronger
checks requested after the finite-grid max-g0 search reached about2.70576.

The direct reference evaluates the original unsimplified A2.7 fractions using
independent scipy Cauchy-PV integration, with no divided differences and no
Legendre-Q formula. Spins0,2,4,10,20 at v=4.001..100 agree to2.76e-11 relative
to1+abs(normalized kernel).

An end-to-end test uses the exactly crossing-symmetric meromorphic amplitude
  a(s,t)=1/(M2-s)+1/(M2-t)+1/(M2-u), g0=3/(M2-c).
Its imaginary measure dmu*b is a unit delta at spin0 and spectral s=M2.
Its REAL partial waves contain infinitely many even spins:
  r_l(s)=rho(s)[2 delta_l0/(M2-s)+8 Q_l(1+2M2/(s-4))/(s-4)].
Keeping real spins0..40 reproduces all24 tested strip identities with residual
<=3.2e-15 for M2=5,60,100. This checks nonzero-spin normalization, the local
real kernel, the source sign, the g0 subtraction, and crossed contributions
together. The test amplitude is for checking the linear dispersive identity;
it is not claimed to satisfy the nonlinear unitarity disk.

The root finite spectral grid originally did not split at new strip endpoints
12,24,48. The kernels have jumps/logarithms there, so every support integration
must add those panel boundaries. Independent endpoint-split reintegration by
the other audit agent shifted the same finite-spin witness to about2.705879,
preserving the candidate improvement but changing its final digits. These are
still finite-spin diagnostics; omitted-spin and all-energy checks remain needed.

Why the high-energy strip has controlled partial-wave convergence:
* |P_l,t|<=P_l,* for every v>4 when -c<=t<=0, controlling absorptive spectral
  sums by the positive g2 measure.
* For real local sums, |H_l|<=1 and r_l^2<=2b_l. Cauchy-Schwarz gives
    integral dmu |pi w H_l r_l|
      <=sqrt(g2)*sqrt(2pi^2 integral dmu w^2 H_l^2/p_l).
  The inverse-Legendre sum converges exponentially on a compact external window
  A>4, so the second factor is finite. This directly controls the local sum and
  does not require extending a full Roy angular projection above s=12.

11. Tail certification status and two discovered witness obstructions
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Do NOT infer a global V/p<=0 assertion from the finite spin/energy scans.

The original untapered high-energy-strip witness has nonzero endpoint weights.
Its Hilbert transforms produce log|mu-A| and log|mu-B| terms with oscillatory
spin-dependent coefficients. The other agent explicitly found positive
divergences (for example spin46 at mu=48), so sup_(tail)(V/p)_+ is INFINITE for
that witness even though its very narrow positive spikes are numerically tiny.
Thus the proposed simple g2*sup(V/p)_+ tail bound cannot certify that witness.

The root then used energy weights (1-x^2)P_n(x), removing these log divergences.
Its initial saved tapered witness had a tiny positive spin2 threshold leading
coefficient and hence a divergent disk support despite an apparently excellent
finite-grid cost. The other agent supplied a small X4-multiplier repair; only
the repaired/tail-validated witness should be used for further inference.

An exact partial certificate is now available:
  python research/certify_roy_dangerous_sign.py WITNESS.npz --output OUT.json
It treats each binary64 multiplier as an exact rational and certifies positivity
of the combined Roy source weight W(s,z) on t<=-4/3 using Bernstein polynomials.
For maxg0_taper1.npz it proves W>=0.0068459077 on this region. The exponentially
growing spectral region |P_l(y)|>P_l(Z) has d=mu-8/3+t<0, hence K>0; this growing
part enters V with a negative sign. This is only one component of the tail proof.

Useful rigorous inequalities for completing the remaining proof:
* In the physical spectral angular interval, put tau=l(l+1)/(mu-4). Then
    max(-1,1-tau|t|)<=P_l(1+2t/(mu-4))<=1.
* Write a=(4/3)tau, lambda_L=L(L+1). The positive Legendre series coefficients
  a_lk=product_(r=0)^(k-1)[1-r(r+1)/(l(l+1))] increase with l. Thus for l>=L,
    P_L(1+2a/lambda_L)<=P_l(Z)<=I0(2sqrt(a)).
  The analogous twice-differentiated lower series gives a lower bound on P_aa.
  Combined with P_a<=I1(2sqrt(a))/sqrt(a), these bound X4/p below via
    X4/p = [tau^2 P_aa/2 -3tau P_a/(2Q)]/P.
* For mu>=49, all external points lie below mu and the direct dispersive K is
  positive. Splitting the source weight into positive/negative parts converts
  the physical-angle inequality above into an explicit conservative envelope.
  research/tail_envelope_exploration.py implements this envelope using ordinary
  source quadrature. At L=100 it is comfortably negative on the sampled compact
  tau range, but its source integrations and continuous parameter enclosure
  HAVE NOT BEEN INTERVAL-CERTIFIED.
* The normalized pure-P_l(Z) subtraction kernel has the especially simple form
    Q^3 K(mu;t,c)=2(t-c)^2/[1-(t-c)^2/Q^2].
* On the nondangerous angular region |y|<=Z, divided differences obey
    |D_l(y,z)|/P_l(Z)<=P_l'(Z)/P_l(Z)<=l/sqrt(Z^2-1).
  Therefore (1/(mu-4))|D|/P_l(Z)<=3l/8 for all mu>4. This removes an apparent
  threshold divergence from naive absolute-value estimates.
* On the dangerous region, Py>=Pstar and K>0 imply L_l/Pstar>=L_0. There
  s-mu>=Q, so the PV-subtracted regular kernel is bounded below by -5/Q.
  Along with the certified positive source weight, this bounds its finite
  potentially positive remainder without retaining any exponential growth.

These estimates reduce a conservative low-energy imaginary tail majorant to
O(l), whereas negative lambda_X4 times X4/p is O(l^2) there. The coarse estimate
can establish a sufficiently remote spin tail; a finite intermediate spin
bridge and continuous energy validation are still outstanding. Consequently
the approximately2.707 tapered result remains a candidate, not an established
new dual exclusion bound at this stage of the work.
